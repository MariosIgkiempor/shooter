# A weapon hits only what it touches

Status: accepted

Melee damage was resolved by `enemy_in_melee_arc`: a cone measured from `origin`, the player's feet anchor, in one instant at Resolve. A Sword killed everything within 60px and 110 degrees of the player's feet, and then played a 0.18s sweep. The two had never agreed, and the disagreement was worse than "approximate". `draw_weapon` begins its swing at `sword_swing_offset(arc, 0)`, which is `-arc/2` — so on the exact frame the damage lands, a Sword's blade is drawn back at **-55 degrees**, pointing away from most of what it just killed, and the sweep the player watches is entirely after the fact. The cone also measured from a different point than the blade was drawn from: `origin` is the feet, `weapon_pivot_position` is chest height, 12px above. Recorded while resolving [Weapon hit volumes](../../.scratch/content-expansion/issues/12-weapon-hit-volumes.md), whose prototype drew both.

**A weapon's hit volume is the weapon itself, tested continuously while it swings.** The volume is an arbitrary set of convex polygons per `Weapon_Kind`, authored in the same unit space as the glyph and hung off the same pivot frame, so it rotates with the drawn shape and can never be somewhere else. It is re-tested every frame across the swing's active window, and across the gap between each frame's poses; a body is damaged once per swing whatever touched it, tracked by a monotonic swing id against a field on `Enemy`. `Melee_Weapon.arc_degrees` is deleted, leaving `Melee_Weapon` with a single size field; the swing arc that drives both the animation and the volume's motion moves to `Weapon_Visual` as a per-kind number.

Alternatives considered. A **swept volume resolved once** keeps the instant model and needs no active window, but it re-tells the same lie in a smaller voice: a body at the far end of the sweep still dies on frame one, before the blade arrives. A **capsule proxy along the blade's spine** is one segment test instead of a polygon test and fattens the tip so nothing whiffs — rejected because the drawn blade is broad at the guard and pointed at the tip, which is the correct property, and a width floor is the proxy returning under another name. Keeping the **cone** was never seriously available for blades, but it survives where it was always honest: `cast_flamethrower_tick` stops borrowing a `Melee_Weapon` and the cone becomes Magic's own, because an emitted flame really is a spreading wedge.

Consequences. The active window was already being reconstructed in the renderer, from `time_since_resolve := (cycle - windup_duration) - cooldown_timer` inside `draw_weapon`; that arithmetic is deleted and **`follow_through_time` carries both fire modes**, which ends its documented status as "purely cosmetic" and ends the rule that Fire mode alone decides whether a weapon Windups *or* Follow-throughs — a Sword now has both. [ADR-0004](0004-windup-fraction-not-duration.md) is untouched: it protects `windup_fraction`'s proportionality, and Follow-through has always been an absolute duration. `Weapon_Visual` stops being cosmetic-only, which is the honest price of the volume being the silhouette; the payoff is that a per-kind arc near zero is a *thrust* rather than a broken sweep, so a Spear needs no machinery of its own. Melee's only family Upgrade, `Arc_Width`, scaled the deleted field: it repoints to `range` and is renamed **Reach**, because scaling `range` scales `weapon_world_frame_size` and grows blade and volume together, while a name outliving its mechanism is how a retired concept returns. Melee stays excluded from the F8 `weapon_visual_scale` slider for the opposite reason to today's — drawn length and reach are now one quantity, so the slider would be a live reach cheat.

Two costs are real rather than incidental. Moving the volume from the feet to the pivot shifts every melee weapon's effective reach 12px up the screen, so all four melee `range` values need a retune, not a rename. And continuous testing introduces tunnelling: a Greatsword crosses its arc in 0.07s, about four frames at 60fps and front-loaded by `ease_out_cubic`, moving its tip **50-90px** on the opening frame — enough for a 16px Mite to sit between two samples and be touched by nothing. Testing the volume between consecutive poses is the answer, which brings the swept model back per frame instead of per swing.
