# Enemies steer by a shared flow field, not per-enemy paths

Status: accepted

Every colliding enemy called `find_path` unconditionally, every frame ([enemy.odin](../../enemy.odin), `chase_to`): a BFS flooded outward from *that enemy* toward its own goal, heap-allocating a `map[Vec2i]Vec2i` and a queue per call, capped at `MAX_SEARCH_NODES :: 1024`, then freeing them and reallocating the enemy's `Path`. At the 24-enemy cap this was fine. [Enemy catalog](../../.scratch/content-expansion/issues/04-enemy-catalog.md) authored rung 4 at **150-250 concurrent bodies**, which puts it at up to 256,000 node visits and ~1000 alloc/free pairs per frame — and `MAX_SEARCH_NODES` was already too small for a path spanning the map's ~2200 walkable cells, so a long path returned not-ok, emptied `enemy.path`, and left the enemy straight-line chasing into walls.

**One flow field is flooded from the player's cell instead, and every enemy reads it.** Four-neighbour, matching the old `get_neighbours`, storing a step direction and a path-distance per cell, over the map's authored cell extent. `find_path`, `Path`, `MAX_SEARCH_NODES` and `Enemy.path` are deleted. It is rebuilt when the player changes cell rather than on a timer or every frame — the goal is a cell, not a point, so this is exact rather than merely fresh, at roughly a tenth of a per-frame rebuild. The field is keyed by inflation radius, built lazily for the radii in use: radius 1 for the roster, and the boss's size-derived radius while a boss is alive, preserving [ADR-0023](0023-enemy-tells-are-absolute-durations.md)-era [Boss model](../../.scratch/content-expansion/issues/05-boss-model.md)'s separate-collision-map decision and its accepted residual.

Alternatives considered. A **staggered BFS budget** — keep per-enemy paths, repath N enemies a frame on a rota — is the smaller change and keeps every behaviour untouched, but it does not fix `MAX_SEARCH_NODES` and it makes chase quality a function of crowd size: at 250 an enemy repaths every ~2 seconds and visibly lags a moving player, so the swarm rung would be the rung where the chase looks worst. **Hierarchical/portal paths** are substantial machinery for one hand-drawn 54x48 map. Inverting the search direction is the decisive property of the chosen option and not merely an optimisation: flooding from the player means an unreachable cell is *never filled*, which is a more useful answer than a failed path, and it removes the truncation constant rather than raising it.

Consequences. Steering splits on whether a Movement Style collides with terrain, the same seam `move_actor` already has: `Grounded`, `Swarmer` and `Charger` read the field; `Floater` never does, keeping euclidean goals, because flying through walls is its identity. A `Ranged` retreat becomes a step to the highest-distance neighbouring cell, which is walkable by construction — it can still back into a dead end, accepted. `Swarmer`'s rotating ring slots are **deleted rather than fixed**: a Swarmer follows the field to its surround radius and drifts along that path-distance contour, which removes `assign_swarmer_slots`'s O(n^2) assignment and makes the two known Swarmer bugs (straight-line seeking, slots placed inside walls by trigonometry) unrepresentable. A cell with no field value — an enemy pushed inside the radius-1 inflation envelope by Separation — falls back to the best-valued of its eight neighbours, reproducing `find_path`'s "endpoints always allowed" exemption without a special case. `Charger` ignores the field for its dash, since a field is a lookup rather than a subscription. `Debug_Visualizer.Pathfinding` keeps its name and toggle and draws the field.

The field must enumerate cells, which forces two things the old search left open. **The world is bounded** to the authored tile extent: `does_cell_collide` treated an absent cell as walkable with nothing beyond the node cap stopping a flood from leaving the map, and 648 of the cells reachable from Desert Dungeon's `player_start` lie outside its authored rectangle. Absent cells *inside* the extent stay walkable, because they are load-bearing today — that map's doorways are untiled gaps and `player_start` itself sits on one, which [Map layout authoring model](../../.scratch/content-expansion/issues/02-map-layout-authoring-model.md)'s already-specified `player_start`-on-floor validity test will catch. And **spawn candidates are tested against the field's filled set**, so a spawn can no longer land in a disconnected pocket and hold [ADR-0017](0017-run-outcome-and-map-objective.md)'s Cleared open forever.

Finally, the field is not the largest per-enemy cost it leaves behind. `move_actor` loops the entire `tilemap.tiles` slice once per axis per actor per frame — 852,000 `CheckCollisionRecs` calls a frame at 250 enemies — which the field does not touch but its cell-indexed solid set fixes for free, testing the ~9 cells an actor's box overlaps. `tile_blocks_point` takes the same fix and `build_inflated_collision_map` becomes a per-Run build rather than a per-frame one. Enemies still do not collide with each other; Separation remains the only mutual force, now sampling a bounded ~8 neighbours rather than every body in its bucket neighbourhood.
