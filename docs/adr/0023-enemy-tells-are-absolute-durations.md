# An enemy's Tell is an absolute duration, not a fraction of its cycle

Status: accepted

Enemies have no telegraph at all today. `Melee` deals its damage the instant `attack_timer` expires while the player is inside `attack_range`, and `Ranged` fires the instant `fire_timer` expires inside its min..max band ([enemy.odin](../../enemy.odin)) — there is no committed action, nothing to react to, and nothing to read. Giving a boss phases that ask the player to *respond* rather than merely to avoid a radius means building enemy-side telegraph machinery from scratch. This ADR settles how that machinery is timed, and names it: an enemy's **Tell** is the visible commitment phase before a telegraphed attack lands.

The obvious move is to copy [ADR-0004](0004-windup-fraction-not-duration.md), which stores a weapon's **Windup** as `windup_fraction` — a proportion of the weapon's current `1/action_rate` cycle — rather than as fixed seconds. That is the settled idiom for "telegraph" in this codebase, and a reader coming to a Tell will ask why it doesn't follow it.

It doesn't follow it because the invariant ADR-0004 exists to protect does not exist on the enemy side. That ADR's whole argument is that `action_rate` upgrades multiply a weapon's rate by 1.10 per pick with no ceiling, so `1/action_rate` shrinks indefinitely; a fixed Windup duration would eventually exceed the cycle it is supposed to nest inside, and the fraction makes the nesting hold by construction. Nothing shrinks an enemy's cycle. An enemy is a *pure stamp* of its **Enemy Preset** ([ADR-0020](0020-enemy-kind-is-the-authored-unit.md)) — nothing scales or modifies it after it spawns, which is the same reason there is no per-Map health multiplier. `attack_cooldown` and `1/fire_rate` are whatever the preset said and stay there for the enemy's whole life.

So a Tell is stored and counted down in **seconds**. Positively, not merely by default: a Tell is something the player has to *learn*, and a learned reaction time has to be stable. Expressed as a fraction, every rebalance of an attack's cooldown would silently retune how long the player has to react to it — tightening the read as a side effect of a pacing change, on the one mechanic where the read is the entire point. Absolute seconds mean the number an author tunes is the number the player experiences.

What does carry over from ADR-0004 is the rule that makes a telegraph honest: **a committed Tell always resolves.** Once it starts it runs to completion and the attack executes, whether or not the player is still in range or still alive to be hit — the same "a committed Windup always resolves" invariant the player's own weapons hold, and the reason a telegraph is a fair warning rather than a bluff. Whether the attack tracks the player through the Tell or locks its target at the start is the per-attack analogue of [ADR-0005](0005-ground-targeted-casts-lock-at-trigger.md)'s split, decided per authored attack rather than globally.

The Tell is not boss-only. It lives on a new **Attack Style** variant available to any **Enemy Kind**, on the bar [ADR-0020](0020-enemy-kind-is-the-authored-unit.md)'s effort set for reopening that axis: a variant must buy a new *player response*, not a stat. A telegraphed committed attack is the first enemy action a player can react to rather than only keep away from, and sharing one piece of machinery means the `Charger` Movement Style's committed dash and the boss's attacks read as the same vocabulary — and that the ladder can teach the read on a lower rung before its top rung demands it.

We considered giving the boss its own bespoke telegraph path outside the axes, since there is exactly one boss. Rejected: it would make the boss's attacks a different animal from every other enemy's for no gain, and it forfeits teaching the read early. We also considered leaving enemy attacks untelegraphed and building the boss's difficulty from volume and health instead — rejected as the thing that makes a boss a big-statted ordinary enemy, which is precisely what the boss was scoped not to be.
