# Shooter

A top-down twin-stick shooter (Odin + raylib). Single global `game` struct, free-function updates (`update_x`/`draw_x`), enums + `[Enum]T` preset tables as the standing idiom for per-kind data.

## Language

**Weapon**:
The player's single currently-equipped combat tool. Common state (kind, fire mode, damage, action rate, cooldown) lives directly on it; type-specific state lives in one of `Gun`, `Melee_Weapon`, or `Magic` behind its `variant` field. See [ADR-0001](docs/adr/0001-weapon-wrapper-struct.md) for why this differs in shape from `Enemy`'s `Movement_Style`/`Attack_Style` bare unions.
_Avoid_: Loadout, armament

**Gun** / **Melee_Weapon** / **Magic**:
The three concrete `Weapon` variants. Gun keeps the clip-and-reload ranged-firing mechanic; Melee_Weapon and Magic are cooldown-only (no stamina/mana economy). A Gun's *reserve* is **deleted**, not merely neutered — a reload refills the clip unconditionally, so a reload is the only interruption a Gun imposes and scarcity of ammunition is deliberately not a mechanic this game has. The reserve survived for a while as a field pinned open by an absurd constant, which is how a retired mechanic keeps its machinery; nothing counts ammunition outside the clip now. Which `Weapon_Kind`s belong to which Weapon family is a separate lookup table — see **Weapon family**.

**Action rate**:
The generic "actions per second" cadence shared by all weapon types — firing (Gun), swinging (Melee_Weapon), casting (Magic). Gates how often `cooldown_timer` lets a weapon act again. **Windup** (when present) is carved out of the front of this same cycle as a proportional slice, not added on top — Action rate stays the true pace of a weapon regardless of whether it Windups, and regardless of how much `action_rate` has grown from upgrades.
_Avoid_: Fire rate (Gun-specific predecessor term, now only correct when talking about Gun specifically)

**Fire mode**:
Whether a weapon's action re-triggers repeatedly while its input is held (`Automatic`) or once per press (`Semi_Automatic`). Applies generically across all three weapon types, not just Gun. Also what decides whether a weapon Windups: `Semi_Automatic` only, see **Windup**. It no longer decides Follow-through, which any weapon may have — a swinging weapon needs one whatever its fire mode, because Follow-through is where its **Hit volume** is live.

**Trigger**:
The player input event that starts a weapon's action cycle — a press for `Semi_Automatic`, each re-fire tick while held for `Automatic`. Distinct from **Resolve** now that `Windup` can delay the effect after the Trigger; the two were interchangeable before Windup existed, since every weapon acted instantly on Trigger.

**Windup**:
The visible telegraph phase a `Semi_Automatic` weapon shows before it acts, sized by the common `Weapon.windup_fraction` preset (0..1, the portion of the weapon's cycle Windup occupies) and driven at runtime by `windup_timer`. Nested inside the weapon's existing cooldown window (see **Action rate**) as a proportional slice, not an absolute duration — expressing it as a fraction of `1/action_rate` rather than fixed seconds means it stays nested automatically no matter how much `action_rate` has grown from upgrades, with no clamping needed anywhere. Once started, a Windup always completes into **Resolve** — it cannot be cancelled by releasing the trigger early. Aim keeps tracking the mouse live throughout for anything aiming along `aim_dir` (Gun, Melee_Weapon, Fireball, Flamethrower, Lightning_Bolt), so the action resolves toward wherever the player is aiming the instant Windup completes, against whatever's actually in range then (it may whiff if the target moved or died) — but a **ground-targeted** cast (Poison_Cloud) is the opposite: its target locks at Trigger and does not track the mouse through Windup at all, see ADR-0005. Was mutually exclusive per weapon with **Follow-through**, gated by Fire mode; a swinging weapon now has both, because its Follow-through is the window its **Hit volume** is live for ([ADR-0026](docs/adr/0026-a-weapon-hits-only-what-it-touches.md)). Everything that resolves in an instant still goes straight from Resolve to Ready.
_Avoid_: Charge, telegraph alone (telegraph is the effect Windup produces, not the name of the mechanic), Windup for the enemy-side equivalent (that is a **Tell**, deliberately a separate term — Windup is weapon-level, see ADR-0003, and is timed as a fraction where a Tell is timed in seconds), windup_time (retired name — Windup is a fraction of the cycle, not a fixed duration; contrast with Follow-through, which is a fixed duration since it has no equivalent invariant to protect)

**Resolve**:
The instant a weapon's actual effect executes — the bullet spawns, the spell casts, the hit-check runs. For a swing, Resolve is where its **Hit volume** goes live rather than where the hit-check finishes: the check then re-runs every frame until the Follow-through ends. For `Automatic` weapons this happens immediately on **Trigger**, same as before Windup existed. For `Semi_Automatic` (Windup-gated) weapons it happens `windup_fraction / action_rate` seconds after Trigger — recomputed fresh at Trigger time from whatever `action_rate` is at that moment — once **Windup** completes.

**Follow-through**:
The phase a weapon shows after Resolve, timed by the common `Weapon.follow_through_time`/`follow_through_timer` fields — the generalized, Weapon-level successor to Melee_Weapon's old `swing_time`/`swing_timer`. Never gates re-triggering; `cooldown_timer` already does that. It **is** the swing's active window: a melee weapon's **Hit volume** is live for exactly this long, so a body entering the arc after Resolve is still hit and a body leaving before the blade arrives is not. Cosmetic for everything that resolves in an instant (a Gun's recoil kick, a Flame Staff's pulse) and load-bearing for anything that swings. Carried by **both** fire modes for that reason, which is the one place Windup and Follow-through are not mutually exclusive — a Semi_Automatic blade Windups *and* then swings.
_Avoid_: Flourish (used while this was still being named; Follow-through is the settled term), swing_time/swing_timer (retired name, now Weapon-level and not Melee-specific), "purely cosmetic" (true until [ADR-0026](docs/adr/0026-a-weapon-hits-only-what-it-touches.md) made it the hit window)

**Hit volume**:
The region a weapon's action actually damages: for anything that swings, *the weapon itself*, so a body is hit only when the weapon touches it. A **set** of shapes rather than one, authored per weapon kind in the same unit space as its glyph and riding the same pivot frame, so it rotates with the drawn silhouette and can never be somewhere else — a weapon may carry several (a head and a haft), or one, and the guard and grip carry none, being the part in the player's hand. Derived from the weapon's kind, exactly as its silhouette is ([ADR-0018](docs/adr/0018-weapon-visual-identity-is-per-kind-not-per-family.md)) — never stored, so it can never disagree with what the player is watching. A swing tests it **live across its whole window**, and across the gap between consecutive frames, rather than once at **Resolve**; a body is damaged once per swing however many shapes touched it. This replaces the retired cone-from-the-player's-feet test, under which a blade damaged ground it visibly never crossed while drawn pointing the other way. An emitted **cone** is not this and keeps its own test — a flame really is a spreading wedge; a blade only ever pretended to be one. See [ADR-0026](docs/adr/0026-a-weapon-hits-only-what-it-touches.md).
_Avoid_: Hitbox (names a body's collision rect, which is a different thing), arc/cone (the retired melee test, and still the honest name for the flamethrower's own), swing range, hit volume for the enemy side (an enemy has no drawn weapon and so has no volume — its reach is **Melee.attack_range**, measured surface to surface)

**Reach**:
Melee's family-specific **Upgrade**, scaling `Melee_Weapon.range`. Named for what it does rather than for the number it touches: `range` sizes the weapon's world frame (`weapon_world_frame_size`), so a stack lengthens the drawn blade and its **Hit volume** together, and the weapon connects further out because it is physically longer. Replaced the retired Arc_Width, which widened the deleted `arc_degrees` — under a blade collider, sweeping the same volume through more ground buys nothing ([ADR-0026](docs/adr/0026-a-weapon-hits-only-what-it-touches.md)).
_Avoid_: Arc width (the retired name, and its mechanism is gone), swing range (already avoided under **Hit volume**), Range (reserved for Magic's own family Upgrade, which extends a cast rather than the weapon)

**Weapon readiness**:
The three mutually-exclusive states a `Weapon` cycles through between actions: **Ready** (`cooldown_timer <= 0`, can Trigger), **Winding Up** (`windup_timer > 0`, Triggered but not yet Resolved), and **Recovering** (`cooldown_timer > 0` but `windup_timer` already 0 — Resolved, or Follow-through playing, but not yet Ready again). A weapon showing Follow-through is always Recovering; a weapon in Winding Up is never Recovering, and vice versa.

**Enemy Kind**:
The authored unit of enemy variety: a named entity — its **Movement Style**, its **Attack Style**, its own `max_health`, its body colour, and the Gold it pays — defined once as an **Enemy Preset** and referred to by name everywhere it is placed. A Map's **Spawn Trigger** composition authors kinds and counts, never behaviours, so an enemy is something the player can learn and a difficulty ladder can order. The behavioural axes stay open alongside it, with an explicit rule for which side a piece of variety belongs on: *behaviour* goes on the axes, *identity* goes on the kind. **Nothing modifies an enemy after it is spawned from its preset** — no affix, no per-rung scaling, no aura, no Run-wide difficulty multiplier. A harder enemy is a different kind, authored and named; anything that adjusts one in flight breaks the thing the kind exists to make learnable, which is that a given enemy dies to a given amount of fire. See [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-enemy-kind-is-the-authored-unit.md).
_Avoid_: Enemy type (informal), Archetype (names a Movement or Attack Style variant, not the composed whole), Elite (a tier considered and rejected — see the **Boss** entry)

**Enemy Preset**:
The authored facts of one **Enemy Kind**, held in the `enemy_presets` table keyed by kind. Deliberately not a full `Enemy`: it carries no world position and no path, only what an author decides. A spawned `Enemy` is a *pure stamp* of its preset plus a position — nothing scales or modifies it afterwards, which is why there is no per-Map health multiplier. The runtime state that cannot be shared (an attack's countdown, a Floater's wobble phase) lives on the spawned `Enemy`'s own copy of the axis values.

**Movement Style**:
An enemy's per-frame steering archetype, held on `Enemy.movement` as its own bare union — how it gets from where it is to where it's going: `Grounded`, ghostly `Floater` drift, `Swarmer` surround, or `Charger` dash. Orthogonal to **Attack Style**, held separately on `Enemy.attack`: an enemy picks one of each independently. `speed` belongs to Movement Style (each variant carries its own), not Attack Style, since it's a movement trait. Sustained speed stays below the player's: nothing on the roster can catch a player who keeps moving, so pressure comes from ground and angles rather than from a footrace. `Charger` is the single exception, and only for the bounded window of its dash. Which pair an enemy gets is no longer authored per Map — it is a property of its **Enemy Kind**, stamped from that kind's **Enemy Preset** at spawn.
_Avoid_: Behaviour alone

**Grounded**:
The Movement Style variant matching today's only movement: chases by reading the **flow field**, colliding with terrain like the player does. Named for what distinguishes it from `Floater` (collides with terrain, and so routes around it) rather than for the chasing behaviour itself, since `Swarmer` also closes on the player via a different route.

**Floater**:
The ghostly Movement Style variant: ignores tilemap collision and drifts erratically rather than beelining, unlike `Grounded`. It never reads the **flow field** — routing around geometry is exactly what it exists to deny — so it aims at the player directly, and backs straight away when it wants distance. Exact drift mechanics (noise/wobble shape, how strongly it's still pulled toward the player) are this map's Floater movement design ticket.

**Swarmer**:
The Movement Style variant that flanks the player instead of converging with other Swarmers on the same point — a genuinely new surround/flank mechanic, not just `Grounded` movement with tuned-up **Separation**. A Swarmer follows the **flow field** until its distance-to-player reaches its own surround radius, then drifts along that contour. The ring is emergent rather than assigned — every Swarmer at one distance, pushed apart by **Separation** — which is why there are no slots to place, and no slot that can land inside a wall. The contour is a *path* distance, so the ring wraps around geometry instead of cutting through it: surrounding the player, never queuing.

**Charger**:
The Movement Style variant that commits: it approaches by the **flow field** below the player's speed, then claims a straight *lane* on the ground for a **Tell** — bearing locked at the moment the Tell starts — then dashes along it faster than the player can run for a bounded distance, then recovers in place. It is the only thing on the roster that outruns the player, and only for the length of the dash, which is what makes the answer a sidestep rather than a footrace; it is also the only Movement Style that asks the player to *dodge* — the others are answered by kiting, closing, or holding position. The lane is drawn where a `Tell_Area` draws its disc, in the same colour, filling as the Tell runs while the body flashes, and is timed in absolute seconds for the same reason; it is as wide as what the dash delivers (the body's contact reach), not as wide as the body. The dash itself carries no damage — the Kind's own **Attack Style** lands when the dash brings it into contact — so it costs no Attack Style slot. The dash ignores the field, and a wall ends it early into the recovery: the map is a tool the player can bait a Charger into.
_Avoid_: Windup (weapon-level; the Charger's commitment phase is a Tell), Charge (avoided under **Tell**, and ambiguous beside this Style's own name)

**Flow field**:
How every terrain-colliding enemy knows which way the player is. One flood outward from the player's cell across the Map's cells, giving each a step direction and a distance-to-player, shared by every enemy rather than searched per enemy — so a crowd of hundreds costs what one enemy costs. Distance is *path* distance, wrapping around walls, which is what lets a **Swarmer**'s ring and a `Ranged` enemy's retreat both fall out of the same structure. A cell the flood never reaches is unreachable from the player, full stop: that is what an enemy standing somewhere it cannot get anywhere from is told, and what stops a **Spawn Trigger** placing an enemy in a pocket it can never leave. `Floater` is outside all of this by design.
_Avoid_: pathfinding, a path, the BFS — there is no per-enemy route any more, and nothing to draw as a line. See [ADR-0025](docs/adr/0025-enemies-steer-by-a-shared-flow-field.md).

**Attack Style**:
An enemy's combat archetype, held on `Enemy.attack` as its own bare union, independent of **Movement Style** — `Melee` (contact damage in range), `Ranged` (fires enemy bullets in a distance band), `Tell_Area` (claims a disc of ground behind a **Tell**, then lands on it), or nil (no attack). Attack Style's `Melee` is unrelated to `Weapon`'s `Melee_Weapon`/the player's Melee weapon family — enemies keep their own separate combat system by design (see the weapon-types map's Out of scope). The axis is open to new variants again: the enemy-behaviours effort froze it, and the content-expansion effort reopened it, on the bar that a new variant must buy a new *player response* rather than a stat. One of the slots that bar allows is spent on a **Tell**-carrying variant: an attack that commits visibly before it lands, so the player can react to it rather than only stay out of its range. That variant covers committed *area* attacks only — `Ranged` stays untelegraphed, since its bullets are the telegraph.

**Tell**:
The visible commitment phase an enemy shows before a telegraphed attack lands — the enemy-side counterpart to a weapon's **Windup**, and deliberately not called that: Windup is weapon-level (ADR-0003) and timed as a fraction of the weapon's cycle, while a Tell is timed in absolute seconds. It is stored in seconds because nothing shrinks an enemy's cycle the way `action_rate` upgrades shrink a weapon's, and because a reaction the player has to *learn* must stay the same length across rebalances of the attack's pacing. Once started, a Tell always resolves into its attack, whether or not the target is still in range — the same committed-action rule Windup holds, and what makes the warning fair rather than a bluff. Carried by an **Attack Style** variant, so it is available to any **Enemy Kind** rather than being boss-only. A Tell belongs to a **committed area attack** — one that claims a patch of ground — and not to ordinary `Ranged` fire, which is already readable from the bullets themselves. Its duration is authored **per attack**, not per **Enemy Kind**, since what makes a duration fair is how far the player must travel to leave that particular attack's area. It shows as the claimed area drawn on the ground at full extent from the first frame, filling as the Tell runs, plus a quickening flash on the enemy's body: the ground says *where*, the body says *when*. See [ADR-0023](docs/adr/0023-enemy-tells-are-absolute-durations.md).
_Avoid_: Windup (weapon-level, and a fraction rather than a duration), telegraph alone (the telegraph is what a Tell produces, not the mechanic's name), Charge

**Separation**:
The steering force that pushes enemies of the same Movement Style apart from each other so they don't clump on the same point or path. Layered on top of Movement Style (blended with the direction that style already wants), never integrated into the **flow field** itself. It samples a bounded number of nearby bodies rather than all of them: the result is a direction, and at swarm density the far end of a crowd cannot change where it points.
_Avoid_: Flocking (the boids term bundles separation with alignment/cohesion, neither of which is in scope)

**Boss**:
The single heaviest **Enemy Kind** on the roster, occupying the top rung of the **Map ladder** and nowhere else. Not a parallel system: it spawns into the ordinary enemy pool through an ordinary **Spawn Trigger**, takes damage, dies and pays out like anything else — which is why killing it satisfies a **Run outcome** of Cleared with no change to that rule, since it is simply the last enemy alive. What distinguishes it is **phases**: health thresholds at which its attack rotation changes. Its **Movement Style** is fixed for its whole life and it never spawns its own adds — adds come from the Map's timeline like every other enemy — so a phase escalates what the player must read, not what the boss is. It is the one entity that keeps a **Resource indicator**.
_Avoid_: Elite (no such tier exists — an elite/affix layer between ordinary enemies and the Boss was considered and rejected, so a heavy enemy is simply an ordinary **Enemy Kind** with more health), Miniboss, Boss fight (the encounter is the rung, not a mode)

**Identity string**:
How every persisted enum value is written and compared: the enum case's own name, never its ordinal. An authored enum's cases can then be reordered and renamed freely, and a file written before the change still means what it said — where an ordinal made declaration order a contract nobody declared, silently handing a save a different weapon or a Map different enemies. A name no case carries is a load failure the reader *reports*, never a value that quietly resolves to whatever now sits at ordinal zero. One parametric pair converts in both directions (`persistence.odin`); the on-disk field is a plain string, and an enum-typed field that needs to survive round-trips carries an identity-string sibling beside it. See [ADR-0028](docs/adr/0028-persisted-enums-are-stored-by-identity-string.md).
_Avoid_: Enum name alone (names the mechanism, not the contract), ordinal, tag, discriminant value

**Weapon family**:
Which group (`Ranged`, `Melee`, `Magic`) a given `Weapon_Kind` belongs to. `Weapon_Kind` stays the single flat enum from [ADR-0001](docs/adr/0001-weapon-wrapper-struct.md); a separate lookup table buckets its members by family. Purely descriptive of the *currently equipped weapon*, not a persistent player choice — the player picks a family fresh at the start of every Run (see **Run**) by picking its tier-0 weapon, and family is derived from whichever weapon that is; nothing on `Player` stores it directly. Still drives the Weapon tier ladder and family-specific Upgrade slots exactly as before — only the permanence is gone. See [ADR-0008](docs/adr/0008-weapon-family-is-run-scoped.md).
_Avoid_: Class (retired name — implied a permanent, once-ever player choice, which is no longer true), Loadout, role, archetype

**Gold**:
The game's single currency, earned from enemy kills (a `Pickup_Kind.Gold` drop, priced per `Enemy_Kind`). Spent in two places that compete for the same wallet: the Shop, on the equipped weapon family's next tier and on repeatable stat Upgrades, both of which last only the current Run; and the Main Menu, on `Account_Stat` and **Relic**, both of which last forever. Account-scoped — it carries across Runs and is never reset by one, which is exactly what makes a Shop purchase cost Account progression: only a Run's *net* take (earned minus spent) banks toward the next Level. See [ADR-0016](docs/adr/0016-gold-is-the-single-currency.md).
_Avoid_: XP (retired — the separate Account-scoped currency Gold absorbed), Currency, coins, cash

**Shop**:
The on-demand UI panel where the player spends Gold, pausing the game while open. Sells two things: the next `Weapon_Kind` in the equipped weapon's family's tier ladder, and repeatable Upgrades (general or family-specific). Opened at the player's discretion mid-Run, unlike the end-of-Run summary screen, which appears once a Run ends and is not player-invoked — see **Run outcome**. Time spent in the Shop is free: the Run's clock stops while it's open, so weighing a purchase against the Map's time limit never itself costs time.
_Avoid_: Store, market

**Weapon tier ladder**:
The fixed, sequential order of `Weapon_Kind`s within a Weapon family, progressed through via Shop purchases (e.g. Ranged: Pistol → SMG → Shotgun → Rifle). Buying the next tier immediately equips it and discards the previous weapon outright — no unlocking a set of owned kinds, no switching back. **Only tier 0 is ever free**: it is what picking a family at the start of a Run equips, and every tier above it is bought. Climbing a tier buys a **different style, not a bigger number** — each family's top tier answers what its lower tiers cannot, so a tier purchase is a decision about how you want to fight rather than a strict improvement. Purchased Upgrade stacks are tracked separately from the equipped weapon and are unaffected by a tier purchase — see **Upgrade**.
_Avoid_: Rank (Level already names the separate Account-progression concept)

**Upgrade**:
A repeatable Shop purchase that raises one stat per purchase, at a rising Gold price, up to a hard per-Upgrade stack cap. Each Upgrade's preset declares whether its per-stack effect is `Additive` (a flat amount, e.g. **Reach**'s +10px) or `Multiplicative` (a proportion, e.g. Clip Size's +15%) — the choice is per-Upgrade, made on whether one number means the same thing across every weapon that can buy it. Either **general** (available regardless of Weapon family — e.g. move speed, max health) or **family-specific** (gated by the currently equipped weapon's family — e.g. Melee's **Reach**). Purchased stacks are Run-scoped but tier-independent: they persist through a Weapon tier purchase, reapplying on top of whichever tier is currently equipped, and only clear at the start of a new Run. Contrast **Account_Stat**, the equivalent purchase but Account-scoped instead of Run-scoped. Bought with Gold, the same wallet `Account_Stat` spends from — buying one is therefore a decision to spend this Run's take rather than bank it ([ADR-0016](docs/adr/0016-gold-is-the-single-currency.md)).
_Avoid_: Stat boost, perk, tier (tier is reserved for the Weapon tier ladder's fixed sequence; an Upgrade's stack count is repeatable, not a sequential ladder)

**Run**:
One attempt at play, from freshly picking a starting `Weapon` and a Map through to whichever **Run outcome** ends it — or until abandoned early. The equipped `Weapon_Kind` and every Upgrade's purchased stack count belong to a Run and reset at the start of the next one. The Gold balance does *not*: it is Account-scoped ([ADR-0016](docs/adr/0016-gold-is-the-single-currency.md)), and what a Run settles into Account progression is only its net take. Every Run begins the same way — picking a starting weapon (`ProgramMode.Run_Start`), then a Map — whether it's the very first Run of a session, the next one after death, or a fresh one chosen from the Main Menu; see [ADR-0008](docs/adr/0008-weapon-family-is-run-scoped.md). A Run already in progress can also be **abandoned**: the Main Menu offers "Continue" (resume it, unchanged) alongside "Start New Run" (discard it and pick fresh) whenever one is in progress; discarding forfeits that Run's weapon tier and Upgrade stacks, but not its Gold — the wallet survives, and a discarded Run simply never settles, so it banks nothing toward the next Level. See [ADR-0013](docs/adr/0013-main-menu-continue-vs-start-new-run.md). Contrast **Account progression**, which survives across Runs.
_Avoid_: Session, game (ambiguous with the global `game` struct), attempt, Restart (still avoided as a name for this — "Start New Run" is reachable only from the Main Menu, never as an in-Playing button, and always begins a new Run rather than replaying the current one; see **Account progression**)

**Run outcome**:
How a Run finished, and the only thing that decides what its Gold is worth. **Cleared** — the Map's Spawn Trigger timeline is exhausted *and* no enemies are left alive — pays the Map's `victory_multiplier` on the Run's net take. Clearing is also the only outcome that leaves a permanent mark: it records the Map as Cleared on the Account, which is what opens the next rung of the **Map ladder**. **Killed** and **Timed_Out** both bank that take at face value; neither forfeits anything, on any rung. Clearing is derived from the timeline rather than from an authored kill quota, because a composition batch is silently truncated at `MAX_ENEMIES`, so a quota can exceed what a Map is ever able to put on the field. A Map whose timeline contains an unbounded `Repeating` Spawn Trigger can never be Cleared. See [ADR-0017](docs/adr/0017-run-outcome-and-map-objective.md).
_Avoid_: Victory/defeat (Timed_Out is neither), win condition, game over (retired screen name), wave clear

**Account progression**:
State that survives across Runs: the Gold wallet, Level, purchased `Account_Stat` and **Relic** stacks, and the set of Maps Cleared at least once (which gates the **Map ladder**, and is keyed by Map **Identity string** rather than by enum ordinal). Level advances on **banked** Gold — a Run's net take, settled once when the Run ends — so it measures what the player chose *not* to spend in the Shop, and a Level is a threshold crossed rather than a reward granted. Unlike the retired XP it replaced, Level is no longer a bare milestone: it gates which `Account_Stat`s are purchasable at all (see that entry). Spending happens on the Main Menu, the same screen Level is shown on (see ADR-0012). No login/profile system backs any of this — "Account" is this repo's chosen name for "survives across Runs" against a single local save file, not a literal user account. See [ADR-0016](docs/adr/0016-gold-is-the-single-currency.md).
_Avoid_: Meta progression, permanent progression

**Account_Stat**:
The permanent stat taxonomy Account progression's Gold buys into, spent between Runs on any of four stats — **Vigor** (Max Health), **Might** (Damage), **Swiftness** (Move Speed), **Fortune** (Gold-gain rate) — all `Multiplicative`. Purchased independently of, and layered underneath, a Run's `Upgrade` stacks: baseline weapon preset → Account_Stat allocations → Run-scoped Upgrade stacks, applied via the same recompute-not-mutate model as `Upgrade` ([ADR-0007](docs/adr/0007-upgrade-stacks-recomputed-not-mutated.md)). Priced on the same geometric mechanism as `Upgrade_Preset`, and now at a comparable growth factor and with a hard `max_stack` — competing for the same Gold as a Shop Upgrade means it can't also be the cheaper, unbounded ladder. Each stat additionally carries an `unlock_level`, so the ladder ahead is bought with banked Gold rather than reached with a single windfall. Fortune is deliberately the shallowest and the last unlocked: it raises Gold gain, and Gold *is* Account progression, so it compounds into its own price.
_Avoid_: Perk, Meta stat (Account progression already names the "survives Restart" bucket this belongs to)

**Relic**:
The permanent *behavioural* taxonomy Account progression's Gold buys into - the sibling of **Account_Stat**'s permanent *numeric* one, bought on the same Main Menu panel, from the same wallet, on the same mechanism (geometric price, hard `max_stack`, Account-Level `unlock_level`). What separates the two is only what a stack buys: an Account_Stat stack is a number folded into an existing stat, whereas a Relic stack puts something in the world that acts on its own. Always-on by definition - a Relic is never triggered, has no cooldown the player manages, and is never equipped or swapped, which is what keeps it off `Weapon`. Unlike Account_Stat it needs no recompute step at all: its live effect is derived from `relic_stacks` every frame rather than applied onto a mutable copy, reaching ADR-0007's discipline by having nothing to keep in sync. The only Relic today is the **Orbiting Orb** - a ring of orbs circling the player, damaging every enemy an orb overlaps on a repeating tick (the same repeating-tick shape as a **Poison_Cloud**'s DoT, not a single hit on entry), where a stack adds a whole further orb rather than scaling the one. Scaled by **Might**, since that is Account progression's Damage stat, but never by the Run-scoped Damage **Upgrade**, which is bought against the equipped **Weapon**. See [ADR-0019](docs/adr/0019-relics-are-a-parallel-account-axis.md).
_Avoid_: Powerup, Passive, Ability (implies something the player triggers - a Relic never is), Perk (already avoided by Account_Stat), Artifact, Item

**Tunable**:
One registered, runtime-editable value: a **slug**, a **Tuning Group**, a pointer to the live variable, a hand-authored slider range, and its **Default**. Registered in `tuning.odin` and edited from the editor's Tuning mode while a Run is paused, so a number can be judged against the thing it changes rather than against a rebuild. Distinct from a **Preset**, and the distinction is the whole point: a Preset (`weapon_presets`, `enemy_presets`, `Relic_Preset`, `Account_Stat_Preset`, ...) is authored game data, whereas a Tunable is the *editing handle* on one of its fields - many Tunables point into a Preset, and the rest point at the loose feel constants that exist as mutable globals precisely so a Tunable can hold their address. An enemy's numbers are on that footing now too: what a Map authors is a Kind and a count, so the sliders that used to sit on a `Spawn_Composition_Entry` are gone and the numbers they edited moved into `enemy_presets` ([ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-enemy-kind-is-the-authored-unit.md)). Only what has an address a Tunable can hold followed them there - a Kind's `max_health` and its Gold; a Movement or Attack Style's own fields sit inside a bare union and are edited in source until the editor grows a Presets mode. A value copied into an entity when it spawned keeps the number it was born with - a Tunable change reaches the player immediately (via the same recompute `Upgrade` uses) but reaches enemies only on their next spawn. See [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-tunables-are-a-registry-over-mutable-globals.md).
_Avoid_: Knob, Setting, Parameter, Config (a Tunable is a single value, not a settings system - there is no user-facing configuration anywhere in this game)

**Default**:
A Tunable's authored in-code value, captured at registration before any **Override** is applied. What Reset restores, and what the sparse save file is compared against. Unrelated to a `Preset`'s "baseline" in the **Account_Stat** sense - that names a position in the Upgrade recompute chain, this names an origin point for editing.

**Override**:
A Tunable whose current value differs from its **Default**, and therefore the only thing written to `data/tuning.json`. Sparse by design: a Tunable left alone is absent from the file, so changing its Default in code still shows through, and the file reads as a list of exactly the decisions that were made. Tracked in git, unlike `data/game_save.json` - a balance pass is a reviewable commit rather than per-machine session state. An Override whose slug no longer matches any registered Tunable is dropped with a warning on load rather than failing it.

**Tuning Group**:
One collapsible category in the editor's Tuning mode, with exactly one expanded at a time. Deliberately fine-grained (a group per weapon kind, per particle burst, per preset table) rather than a handful of broad domains: only the expanded group emits UI nodes, which keeps a frame's node count inside the layout engine's budget — all ~275 Tunables at once would be roughly 1700 nodes against a cap of 1024 — and makes the group list itself the navigation. The list scrolls (`vendor/ui` gained scroll containers), so the accordion is a node-budget device now rather than the only way to reach a group off the bottom of the screen.

**Map**:
A named, reusable level definition: tile layout, a Spawn Trigger timeline, a player start position, and the objective it is cleared against (a `time_limit` and the `victory_multiplier` clearing it pays). Stored as its own file under `data/maps/`, and loaded into the game's runtime state at session start. The same struct shape serves both roles — the on-disk file and the live, mutable copy a session plays on — so no separate blueprint/runtime type exists; loading a map copies its data fresh into runtime state, which means in-session mutation (destructible tiles, ticking Spawn Trigger timers) never touches the file, and reloading the file always resets it.
A Map is a **place**: its layout is drawn by hand and is the same every time it is played, which is what lets a player learn it. What happens *on* it is not fixed — enemies arrive from a random off-screen bearing, so geometry is learnable while pressure is not, and there is no Run seed. Layouts are never generated; see [ADR-0021](docs/adr/0021-map-layouts-are-hand-authored-places.md). A valid Map is checked rather than eyeballed, by a sweep over the baked `maps` table that fails the build: one connected walkable region, a player start on an authored floor tile, a reachable off-screen spawn ring, a cell extent within `MAP_MAX_CELL_EXTENT`, a rung that with the other Maps' covers the ladder once each, authored colours with the floor darker than the wall, and a `time_limit` that clears the earliest its timeline can finish (or is untimed). Footprint stays roughly uniform across Maps — a harder Map is denser, not bigger — which is what the extent bound holds. Every Map also carries its position on the **Map ladder** (see that entry) as an authored `rung`, alongside the objective fields, rather than taking its order from the `Map_Name` enum — that enum is generated in filename order, which is nobody's chosen ordering. A Map likewise carries its own **Map theme** (see that entry): its visual identity is authored data on the Map, not a presentation table kept alongside it.
_Avoid_: Level (already names the player's Account-progression level, see **Weapon tier ladder**), Blueprint, Room (informal — a map may contain multiple rooms, not itself a modeled concept)

**Map ladder**:
The ordered sequence of Maps, one per **rung**, that a player works up. A rung is a whole Map — a Run is one attempt at one rung, never a chain of them — and it is playable only once the rung below it has been Cleared at least once, rung 1 always being open. Gating on clears rather than on Account Level is deliberate: Level measures banked Gold, so it would ask whether the player had saved enough rather than whether they could handle the fight. What escalates up the ladder is the enemy roster and the density and overlap of Spawn Triggers, with layout hostility a secondary axis; what stays flat is footprint, Run length, and enemy health. A rung's `victory_multiplier` rises only shallowly, because a denser roster already drops more Gold on its own, and failing a rung still banks a Run's net take at face value — the cost of an attempt is the time and the forgone bonus, never the wallet. See [ADR-0022](docs/adr/0022-map-rungs-are-gated-by-clears.md).
_Avoid_: World, Stage, Chapter, Difficulty setting (a rung is a distinct place, not a modifier applied to one), Wave (already avoided by **Spawn Trigger**)

**Map theme**:
A Map's visual identity: an authored **floor colour** and **wall colour**, plus the set of **ambient effects** it runs. Everything else derives — the wall bevel is mixed between the two, the accent that tints the ambience is pushed off the wall colour, and the swatch the Map Selection screen draws *is* the wall colour, so the menu cannot advertise a colour the world does not have. A theme is authored on the Map itself rather than in a presentation table beside it, because a theme kept elsewhere is a theme a new Map can silently be missing; see [ADR-0024](docs/adr/0024-a-map-owns-its-visual-identity.md).
A theme does not touch how tiles are drawn. Floor and wall stay flat fills read off `Tile.collides`, with a darker inset bevel on walls and no grid lines or per-tile colour variation — a theme only chooses the colours those rules use, and layers effects above and below the actors. Colour alone does not make a Map a distinct place, which is why the ambient set is authored per Map and not fixed: five Maps differing only in hue read as five palettes rather than five places.
_Avoid_: Biome (implies generated terrain, which [ADR-0021](docs/adr/0021-map-layouts-are-hand-authored-places.md) rules out), Tileset (nothing is tiled from an atlas any more — tiles are flat shapes), Skin (implies an interchangeable layer over fixed content; a theme belongs to one Map)

**Ambient effect**:
A code-driven, purely decorative layer a **Map theme** runs continuously: drifting motes in the air above the actors, large off-grid patches on the floor beneath them, and a directional light wash over the place. Ambient effects carry no gameplay information and never occlude any — which is why the darkened-edge vignette was rejected, since the screen edges are exactly where enemies enter. They are distinct from **particles**, which are event-driven, bounded by what just happened, and cleared at a Run boundary; ambience is steady-state and outlives the Run, so it keeps its own fixed budget rather than competing with hit feedback for one (`AMBIENT_MOTE_MAX` and `AMBIENT_PATCH_MAX` in `ambience.odin`, never `game.particles`). The no-occlusion rule also fixes each layer's slot in the world pass: patches at the top of the **Ground layer**, the wash between the tilemap and the Ground layer so it lights the place and tints nothing drawn on it (a world-space quad, so a menu's blurred backdrop blurs it too), motes above the bodies but under damage numbers and resource indicators. A Map that runs none looks exactly as its tiles do.
_Avoid_: Weather, Effect (unqualified — already overloaded by weapon and Relic effects), Decoration

**Pickup**:
A collectible dropped where an enemy died, on a per-death roll. Two kinds: **Gold**, priced from the dying **Enemy Kind** at spawn time so a drop is worth what it came from, and **Health**. There is deliberately no third — an Ammo pickup existed while a Gun had a reserve to refill and was retired with it, and a pickup that restores nothing is worse than no pickup, because it teaches the player to walk toward things that do not matter. New kinds are cheap to add and each one dilutes the roll, so the roster is held at what a Run actually spends.
_Avoid_: Drop (fine as a verb, ambiguous as a noun — a boss's guaranteed payout and a rolled one are both drops), Loot (implies inventory, which the game has none of)

**Ground layer**:
The world-space draw slot immediately above the tilemap and beneath every actor, holding a **Map theme**'s floor patches and a **Tell**'s claimed area. It is defined by *whose information it carries*, not by lying flat: what belongs here is the world telling the player something — scenery, or ground an enemy has claimed — so nothing on it may be hidden by a crowd standing on it, because a crowd standing on it is the case that matters. The player's own output stays above the actors: a poison cloud is feedback on a shot already fired and reads as the player's, not the world's, which is why it is drawn over the bodies it is killing rather than under them.
_Avoid_: Decal layer (too narrow — a Tell is not decoration), Background (the tilemap is the background; this is above it)

**Spawn Trigger**:
One entry in a `Map`'s spawn timeline, replacing the old fixed-position `Spawner` (no position is ever authored — enemies always spawn off-screen relative to the camera, computed dynamically at spawn time, never from a placed point). Holds three orthogonal fields, mirroring how **Movement Style**/**Attack Style** split `Enemy` into sibling axes rather than one bundled variant: a **Condition** (`Time_Elapsed(seconds)` or `Kills_Reached(count)`, the latter read against the Run's cumulative kill total — the same value the live HUD counter shows, not a separate per-map count) that gates when the trigger first activates; a **Mode**, either `One_Shot` (spawns its composition once, then is done forever) or `Repeating` (spawns its composition every `interval` seconds, optionally for a bounded `duration` before stopping — an unbounded `Repeating` trigger runs for the rest of the Run, and therefore makes its Map unclearable — see **Run outcome**); and a **Composition**, a list of `(Enemy Kind, count)` entries spawned together as one batch, generalizing `Spawner`'s single fixed template into a mix. A Composition names kinds and nothing else — it cannot tune an enemy, which is what keeps a kind the same enemy wherever it appears (see [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-enemy-kind-is-the-authored-unit.md)). A Condition is checked once, at the moment it's first satisfied — a Spawn Trigger never re-fires or re-evaluates after activating, and multiple triggers (including several concurrently-running `Repeating` ones) may be active at once, layering pressure the way Vampire-Survivors-style waves stack.
_Avoid_: Spawner, Wave (no dedicated "wave" concept exists — pacing comes entirely from independent Spawn Triggers stacking, not a single ordered wave list)

**Resource indicator**:
A small icon-plus-particle readout drawn in world-space above an entity — the same above-entity placement `draw_health_bar` once used for Enemies, now the only place any entity's resources are shown. The player always shows a **Health indicator**. Ordinary enemies no longer show one: their remaining health is read from the body's opacity, which fades as they weaken. The **Boss** is the exception and keeps a Health indicator, because opacity cannot resolve a fraction finely enough on an entity the player spends a minute killing — an exception to which entities carry an indicator, never to the rule that no indicator is screen-space. The player additionally shows exactly one secondary Resource indicator at a time, keyed by the equipped Weapon's family: an **Ammo indicator** for Gun (clip level — there is no reserve to show — plus a treatment for reload distinct from plain depletion) or a **Cooldown indicator** for Melee_Weapon/Magic (`cooldown_timer` counting back to Ready — see **Weapon readiness**). Windup gets no Resource indicator of its own — it already has a dedicated visual telegraph on the weapon's own draw (arc pull-back for Melee_Weapon, pullback along `aim_dir` otherwise), so a Resource indicator would be redundant with it. Every Resource indicator is fraction-only: no numeric readout.
_Avoid_: HUD bar, resource bar (retired names for the removed screen-space bars — a Resource indicator is world-space and icon+particle-driven, not a filled bar; HUD itself still applies to the menu screens, which are unaffected)

**Icon**:
A shape-drawn glyph identifying a thing in the UI — a Weapon kind, an Upgrade, an Account_Stat, Gold, a Map. Drawn from code, never from the atlas (there is no icon art in the atlas), and authored in a **unit square** so one definition serves both the 9px **Resource indicator** slot and the ~20px menu-row slot; see `icon.odin`. An Icon draws in its subject's own world colors so it matches the thing it depicts, unless given a **tint**, which *replaces* every part of the glyph with one flat color — that's how a maxed, unaffordable or locked row grays out. Tint is distinct from alpha, which always applies on top and carries **Reveal** / **Dismiss** motion rather than state. Icons sit *above* their label on anything the player browses and picks between, and inline to the left on read-only `label: value` readouts.

Weapon Icons are per-`Weapon_Kind`, not per-**Weapon family**, and the *same* geometry is what `draw_weapon` renders in the world — an **Icon frame** decides where a glyph's unit square lands, either upright in a menu slot or anchored at the weapon's grip and rotated to the player's aim (mirrored past vertical, so a grip never hangs in the air). Per-kind sizes and effect magnitudes live in `weapon_visuals`, derived from `kind` at draw time and never stored on **Weapon**. See [ADR-0018](docs/adr/0018-weapon-visual-identity-is-per-kind-not-per-family.md).
_Avoid_: Sprite, texture, atlas tile (all imply the retired art pipeline — an Icon is geometry, not an image), Glyph on its own (collides with the font's own character glyphs)

**Screen**:
One of the six full-window menu UI states the game can be in — Splash, Main Menu, Run Start, Map Selection, Run End, Shop — each with its own draw function in `hud.odin`. Distinct from **Run**: a Run is a play attempt; several Screens are shown before a Run starts and after it ends, but none of them are the Run itself, and none are shown while actually Playing. The gameplay HUD (**Resource indicator**) is not a Screen — it's a persistent world-space overlay shown continuously while Playing, not a full-window state the player enters and leaves. A **Screen change** — switching which Screen is current — Dismisses every Menu element on the outgoing Screen and Reveals every Menu element on the incoming one; see **Reveal** / **Dismiss**.
_Avoid_: Menu (ambiguous — a Screen is the whole window state, not the panel/dropdown sense "menu" usually implies), Mode (the underlying code enum is `ProgramMode`, but "Mode" alone is too generic for the glossary)

**Blurred backdrop**:
The game world rendered blurred and dimmed behind a **Screen** instead of sharp, whenever that Screen is showing over a populated world (`current_screen()` non-nil and `current_map.name` non-empty) — today, exactly Shop and Run End, the only two Screens with a world behind them. Driven by `blurred_backdrop_strength()` (`hud.odin`), which returns the same rank-0 alpha the Screen's own panel **Reveal**/**Dismiss** animation already computes rather than a separately-tuned timer, so the blur fades in and out in lockstep with the panel. See [ADR-0015](docs/adr/0015-blurred-backdrop-reuses-panel-reveal-dismiss-alpha.md).

**Menu element**:
Anything on a Screen capable of its own **Reveal** / **Dismiss** — a panel, a button, or the Splash background image. Not every visual on a Screen necessarily needs to be one (e.g. text drawn as part of a panel could just inherit its parent panel's timing rather than animating independently) — which visuals get their own Menu element vs. inherit their parent's is this map's Component API ticket to decide.

**Reveal** / **Dismiss**:
The two phases a Menu element animates through when it starts or stops being shown: Reveal is the fade-in as it becomes visible, Dismiss is the fade-out before it's removed. Each Menu element carries its own **Reveal delay** — an offset before its Reveal starts, relative to when its Screen change begins. `0` for every element on a Screen makes that Screen's Reveal look synchronized (today's default expectation); staggering the delays across a Screen's elements produces a cascading reveal instead (e.g. buttons appearing one after another) — the same mechanism, not a separate one.
_Avoid_: Enter/Exit, Show/Hide (too generic — could mean instant visibility toggling, not an animated phase), Transition (describes the Screen-change event, not a per-element phase)
